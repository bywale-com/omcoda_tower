> **First pass — June 14 2026.** This is the initial architecture of the opt-in launch strategy for Tower. The sequence is designed to maximise activation through authority transfer — the firm co-signs Tower to its existing client base, which means the PCRE score is already high before a single message is sent. The client isn't being asked to trust Tower. They're being told their firm already does.
> 

---

## Context

The opt-in launch is the first launch in the Tower system. Its job is to move existing clients from a dormant relationship with the firm into an active, expectation-set relationship with Tower. The authority that makes this work is already banked — the firm has an open file with each client. The launch doesn't establish credibility. It inherits it.

**Core principle:** Don't squander the authority that already exists. Make it feel official. Make it feel like the firm is extending something — not selling something.

---

## Launch Window

5–7 days total.

---

## Sequence

### Day 1 — Text primer

- **Channel:** SMS
- **Message:** Brief, official-feeling text from the firm. Signals that an important email is coming today. Asks for a Y reply to confirm receipt.
- **Purpose:** Primes the inbox. Forces attention toward the email before it lands. Even if the email isn't read, the client has now seen the firm's name twice before the call.

### Day 1 — Announcement email

- **Channel:** Email
- **Design:** Klaviyo-level production. Two logos — the firm and Tower side by side. Official partnership framing.
- **Content:** Warm but authoritative. Firm introduces Tower as a Toronto-born startup doing X for immigrants pursuing Canadian permanent residence. Positions this as a value-added service being extended to ongoing clients. Announces a waitlist and a launch date. Feels like a real partnership event, not a newsletter.
- **Purpose:** Establishes Tower with full institutional backing. Transfers the firm's authority to Tower in one send.

### Day 2 — First friction touchpoint

- **Channel:** Social (Instagram or LinkedIn)
- **Action:** Ask clients to follow the firm's page. Or Tower's page. Something anyone can do in 10 seconds.
- **Purpose:** First micro-yes. Low commitment, high signal. Anyone who does it is leaning in. Also builds social proof passively.

### Day 3 — Waitlist / landing page

- **Channel:** Email + SMS
- **Action:** Direct clients to a landing page to join the waitlist for Tower access.
- **Purpose:** Converts passive interest into an active commitment. Waitlist signup is the first real opt-in signal. Also introduces natural scarcity — slots are limited, launch date is set.

### Days 4–7 — Nurture sequence

- **Channel:** 2 emails + 1 text over remaining days
- **Content:** Light education about what Tower does and why it matters for their specific situation. If video content is produced — firm's lead consultant talking about their client profile and where they see Tower helping. Not Tower-branded content. Firm voice throughout.
- **Purpose:** Keeps the launch alive between announcement and go-live. Builds anticipation. Surfaces any remaining questions before the sequence ends.

---

## Email Design Standard

All emails in this sequence should meet Klaviyo-level production quality. Clean layout, firm branding, Tower co-branding where appropriate. Official but not cold. The tone is institutional and warm — a firm communicating to people whose files are already open.

---

## Success Metrics

These metrics are observable within the first 48–72 hours and tell you whether the cohort is alive before the full sequence completes.

| Metric | What it measures | Signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMS delivery rate | Reachability of the list | Baseline hygiene check |
| SMS Y-reply rate | Immediate engagement | High reply = high PCRE cohort |
| Announcement email open rate | Authority of the firm's email | Below 30% = deliverability or relationship problem |
| Announcement email click rate | Active interest beyond open | Click = leaning in |
| Waitlist conversion rate | Intent to participate | Primary success metric for opt-in |
| Social follow rate | Micro-yes completion | Leading indicator of reactivation responsiveness |
| Nurture email open rate (days 4–7) | Sustained interest | Drop-off here = sequence fatigue or wrong cohort |
| Unsubscribe rate | Relationship health | Spike = tone or frequency problem |

**Reading the metrics:**

Open + click > open only. Open only > no open. Anyone who opens, clicks, follows, and joins the waitlist is in the top tier of the cohort — these are the clients most likely to convert at reactivation. Segment them early.

---

## Notes

- Video content is not required. If produced, it is firm-voiced — the lead consultant talking about their client profile and the problem Tower solves. Not a Tower product walkthrough.
- The waitlist creates real scarcity. Do not manufacture urgency on top of it.
- Tower stays behind the curtain during the launch. The firm is the face. Tower is the infrastructure being extended.

---

## Launch Day

Launch day is when the actual sign-up link goes out — not the waitlist. The waitlist was anticipation. This is the real opt-in. They are signing up to receive nudges and be part of the Tower system.

**This sign-up moment has three jobs:**

1. **Set expectations** — what Tower is, what it will do, how often it will show up, what the nudges look like
2. **Establish consequences** — by signing up they are opting into a paradigm. Not just a service. A relationship with defined terms. The consequences of not engaging were established here, at sign-up, not later.
3. **Plant the reactivation seed** — framed not as "we'll chase you when you're eligible" but as "when we have time for you, we'll let you know." The window belongs to Tower. This framing must feel natural, not salesy.

**Launch day sequence:**

- Email with sign-up link goes out
- Landing page — clean, sets expectations and consequences, reactivation framing embedded naturally
- Confirmation email on sign-up — welcomes them, reinforces what they've joined, sets the first expectation of what comes next

## Reactivation Sequence — First Pass

Triggered when Tower detects service eligibility for a client already in the system.

**Core principle:** We're not asking them to come on the calendar. We're telling them we now have time for them. The slot belongs to Tower. They secure it.

---

### Day 0 — Text primer

- **Channel:** SMS
- **Message:** Something has come up in your file. We've identified something worth discussing. Look out for an email later today. Reply Y to confirm you received this.
- **Purpose:** Primes the inbox. Forces attention. Y-reply is a data point — intent signal before a single email lands. Non-reply is also a data point. Both inform what comes next.

---

### Day 0 — The email

- **Channel:** Email
- **Content:** Tell them what Tower observed. Short. They may be eligible for something. No hard pitch. The email includes a link to their no-login client view.
- **No-login client view:** A read-only online view of their profile — pathway status, what was detected, where they stand. No account required. They click the link, they see their file. This makes the eligibility real and visible before the ask is made. They're not being told something abstract. They're seeing it.
- **Close of email:** In a couple of days, a slot will open for them to schedule a time. They don't need to do anything right now. Just know it's coming.
- **Purpose:** Establish the opportunity as real and specific. Plant the slot framing — the window is opening, not the other way around.

---

### Days 1–3 — Nurture toward the slot

- **Channel:** 2 emails over 3 days
- **Content:** Open them up to what the call looks like, what happens on it, what they can expect. For first-time reactivations especially — this is orientation, not pressure. What does a qualification call look like? What will be covered? What's the outcome if they qualify?
- **Purpose:** Remove friction from the booking decision. By the time the slot opens, they know exactly what they're booking and why. No surprises.

---

### Day 3 — Slot opens

- **Channel:** Email + SMS
- **Action:** The booking link goes live. Framed as: your slot is now available. Limited availability. Here's how to secure it.
- **Purpose:** Convert the warmth built over 3 days into a booked meeting. The slot opening is an event, not a reminder.

---

## Metrics — Reactivation Sequence

| Metric | What it measures | Signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMS Y-reply rate | Immediate engagement on trigger | High = warm cohort |
| SMS non-reply rate | Disengagement or unreachable | Flag for channel switch |
| Email open rate | Authority and subject line strength | Below 35% = relationship has cooled |
| No-login view click rate | Real interest in their own eligibility | Click = leaning in strongly |
| Time spent on view | Depth of interest | Longer = higher intent |
| Nurture email open rate | Sustained engagement over 3 days | Drop-off = friction in the process |
| Slot booking rate | Conversion — the primary metric | This is the outcome |
| Booking drop-off rate | How many booked but didn't show | Follow-up protocol needed |

**Reading the signal:** Y-reply + view click + nurture open + booking = top-tier reactivation candidate. Each step that completes without the next following tells you exactly where the friction is.
