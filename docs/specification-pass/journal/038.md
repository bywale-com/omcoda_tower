## The in-page anchor does not add a formula, and the FAQ next to it does name the day

`deliv-11` says to measure complaint rate per reputation unit against a hard ceiling of about 0.3 percent and a target at or under 0.1 percent, throttle as the rate approaches the target, and quarantine when the ceiling is breached. The sender page was fetched again, this time as HTML, because the previous markdown fetch had dropped the link targets. “Learn more about spam rates” is `href="#spam-rate"`. It is not another article. The section it opens repeats the bars: keep the Postmaster rate below 0.10 percent and avoid ever reaching 0.30 percent or higher. It links Postmaster Tools and does not state a numerator, a denominator, or a window. That is why the previous session could not fill the two choices from the bars alone.

The same help cluster links the Email sender guidelines FAQ at `https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14229414`. That page asks what time range is used when calculating spam rate, and it answers that the rate is calculated daily. It says a user-reported rate over 0.3 percent makes a bulk sender ineligible for mitigation while the rate stays over that line, that the figures are updated daily in Postmaster Tools, and that eligibility returns when the rate has remained below 0.3 percent for seven consecutive days. The Postmaster dashboards page at `https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14668346` says the tool uses Coordinated Universal Time, and that the spam rate is the percent of messages delivered to an engaged recipient’s inbox and then marked spam by that recipient. Mail the recipient moves from spam into the inbox counts as inbox mail. The displayed series is DKIM-authenticated mail to Gmail accounts. A low-volume day may show no point, for privacy. The page also says third-party spam reports will not match that series, and that a rate can look low when Gmail has already filed the mail as spam, because those messages never sat in the inbox to be marked.

The day-versus-month choice the handoff left open is the FAQ’s day. A month is not the grain the bars are quoted from. The delivered-versus-sent choice is the same page’s inbox delivery, not the count of mail accepted for send. The seven-day sentence is how long a ceiling breach keeps the sender out. It is not a second window for the 0.1 percent band.

## The gate can compute a ratio, and it cannot see an engaged Gmail inbox

`handleResendWebhook` maps `email.complained` to `complaint` and `email.delivered` to `delivered`, and it stamps `at` with `now()` at the insert. The day of a receipt is therefore the UTC date of that timestamp, not the day `outbound_messages` was created. A late complaint counts on the day it arrives. `provider_webhook_receipts` already drops a replay before the class is handled, so the ratio does not count a duplicate provider event. Two stored events for one `message_id` are still one message. `firm_id` `unknown`, used when the outbound lookup misses, is not a sending domain and is not mixed into a real firm’s counts.

`accepted` cannot be the denominator. `dispatchEmailLive` inserts `accepted` when the send is handed to Resend, `dispatchSmsLive` inserts `accepted` for the text, and `email.sent` maps to `accepted` as well. Dividing by that class would mix the text into an email bar and would count mail that had only been accepted, which is the quantity the dashboards page says the rate is not. `delivered` is the delivery receipt the handler already stores. It is wider than “engaged recipient’s Gmail inbox”: the webhook does not say the folder, the mailbox provider, or that the message was DKIM-signed. `deliv-12` is the Postmaster sample, and nothing in `server/` ingests it. Waiting for that sample would leave the next send ungated, which is the opposite of the `deliv-11` path and of `deliv-14`. The bars are applied to the ratio these two event classes can form. The Postmaster percentage and this ratio will differ. That difference is part of the claim, not a reason to leave the rate unset.

A day with no `delivered` row has no ratio. Treating a lone complaint that day as an infinite rate would quarantine a quiet day the percentage cannot describe. The address deny from claim 50 still suppresses the complained address. The unit does not stop.

There is no volume floor. Google’s 5,000-message line is who it calls a bulk sender. The missing dashboard point on a small day is a privacy cut. `deliv-11` states the discipline as the two percentages. One complaint is 0.30 percent of 334 deliveries and more than that of any smaller count, so a small book meets the ceiling on a single complaint when that day’s deliveries are under 334. Adding a floor would be a third bar, and it would make claim 50’s “one complaint is not the quarantine” true in a stronger sense than the percentage allows. The event is still not the quarantine. The ratio is. On a day of 1,000 deliveries, one complaint is 0.10 percent and throttles. It does not meet the ceiling.

The unit is the `firm_id` already on the events. That is the firm the outbound row named, and `sending_domains` has one row for it. No IP-tier table exists, so the rate does not invent a second counter. Another firm’s mail is not this denominator, and this firm’s breach does not stop the peer. `handlePoolAllocate` can rewrite pool columns on the same id. It does not split the count.

## Throttle is the rest of the day, and quarantine is the seven

The check belongs in `dispatchEmailLive`, after consent and halt have allowed the send and after claim 50’s address join would have returned `suppressed`. A suppressed address still reports `suppressed`. A clean address on a hot unit does not reach `emails.send`. The return is `{ ok: false, deny: "policy" }`, with detail `complaint-throttle` or `complaint-quarantine`, so the words are not `silenced`, `halt active`, or `suppressed`. Claim 21’s writers do not read `detail`. The engagement kind stays `send_denied`. No outbound row and no `accepted` event are written by that return. The attempt stays `open`. This is not claim 20’s halt abort and not claim 9’s meeting.

Quarantine is true when any of the seven UTC dates ending today has a defined rate of 0.30 percent or higher. That is the FAQ’s “while over 0.3 percent,” read as a window the send path can compute without a new column: the bad day holds the unit until it ages out, and a later day that also breaches holds it again. A day with no deliveries neither clears the window nor extends it. Throttle is today’s rate at or over 0.10 percent and under 0.30 percent, and only when the seven-day predicate is false. It lifts at the next UTC date unless that date leaves the target. Stopping further email is the cap. A fraction of the book that might still send is not on `deliv-11` or on the FAQ, so it was not invented. `deliv-23` remains the deferral backoff. It is a different signal.
