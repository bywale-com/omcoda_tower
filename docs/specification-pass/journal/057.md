On success it stores `outbound_messages.status` `accepted` and a `messaging_events` row of class `accepted`. `routes.ts` posts `/webhooks/resend` only. The wire app is mounted at `/wire` and at `/api/wire`. A carrier post has nowhere to land. ## What Twilio actually posts

The message-resource page lists `queued`, `sending`, `sent`, `failed`, `delivered`, `undelivered`, and several Messaging Service and inbound values. `failed` is a failure to send, including queue overflows, account suspensions, and media errors. `undelivered` is a delivery receipt that the message was not delivered, and the same sentence names carrier content filtering and the availability of the handset as two causes of that one status. `StatusCallback` is a create parameter. The status-callback guide says the initial status does not generate a callback. A message created with `From` and without a Messaging Service starts at `queued`. The live create is that shape: `twilioFromNumber()` is one env number, and the call does not pass `MessagingServiceSid`. The `accepted` row the create already writes is that initial queue. A second row for it would invent a callback the guide says is not sent.

The callback that does arrive carries `MessageStatus` and, on `failed` or `undelivered`, `ErrorCode`. The incoming-message page is a different post, form-urlencoded, with `From`, `To`, and `Body`. The advanced opt-out page says Twilio’s default long-code keywords are STOP, UNSUBSCRIBE, END, QUIT, STOPALL, REVOKE, OPTOUT, and CANCEL, matched without regard to case. `OptOutType` of STOP, START, or HELP is added when Advanced Opt-Out is on, which is a Messaging Service feature this sender does not configure. `START` and `UNSTOP` are the reserved words that undo a block. `YES` does not. A blocked number’s later send fails with error 21610. HELP does not change the block list.

The error pages split the one word `undelivered`. 30003 is an unavailable handset and says to retry. 30005 says the number may no longer exist and also says the device may be off, the line may be a landline, or the carrier may be failing, and it says to send another test. 30006 says the destination cannot receive the message, names a landline, and says not to retry that SMS. 30007 says Twilio or the carrier filtered the message, names spam and policy, and the remedy is the sender’s compliance plus a support ticket with three SIDs. It does not say the handset is dead.

That split is why `deliv-24`’s one sentence could not be applied to every undelivered callback. Suppressing the number whenever the status is `undelivered` would block a phone that was powered off. That is the mistake claim 55 already refused for a temporary mail bounce. The paper’s reputation unit would have been the other home for 30007, and there is no SMS percent on `deliv-11` or on the 30007 page to turn one filter into a firm throttle. Claim 53’s bars are the email spam rate. Using class `complaint` for 30007 would have put a text into that numerator.

## What was settled

The route is `POST /webhooks/twilio` on the wire app. The signature uses the Twilio validator and the auth token. A missing token is 401, not the Resend fail-open. The receipt id is the message SID plus the status, or plus `inbound`, so one SID can carry several statuses and a replay returns before the class is applied. When a public origin is set, the create passes `statusCallback` at `/api/wire/webhooks/twilio`. When it is not set, the create still omits the callback, and a console URL can post to the route anyway.

`failed` other than 21610 is `rejected`, which is claim 84, and it still does not suppress or pause. `sent` and `queued` are stored and gate nothing. `delivered` is stored as `delivered`, and claim 53’s ratio now counts a message only when the outbound channel is email, so a text does not become an email delivery. The earlier sentence that SMS `accepted` rows stay out of the ratio still stands. 30006 is `sms_unreachable` and joins the next text, in every firm, the way claim 50 joins an address, with detail `suppressed`. It does not silence the pair. Silencing would stop that contact’s email because a landline cannot take a text, which is the email rule `deliv-24` kept off SMS. 30007 is `sms_filtered` and does not join. 30003, 30005, and an undelivered callback with no code are `sms_undelivered` and do not join. `read` is not the attempt’s `opened`.

STOP, the other default opt-out keywords, `OptOutType` STOP, and error 21610 are class `stop`. The phone compare is the last 10 digits, because the product’s SMS path is Canadian and Twilio sends E.164 while the book’s phone is unparsed text. The event hangs on the newest SMS outbound to that number. The contact on that row is silenced with source `sms-stop`, and the dispatch is claim 15’s `consent_records.opt_out`, because `casl-11` says the keyword is the Silence button and the button dispatches. A hard bounce still does not. Another firm’s contact is not silenced by the phone. Claims 18 and 23 already refused that join. The shared from-number is why the next text is denied in every firm anyway. `START` and `UNSTOP` lift the `stop` join and do not clear `silenced` and do not lift 30006. Express consent remains the Agree write `casl-15` already required. `YES` does not lift. HELP changes nothing. A keyword that arrives before any outbound row has no phone to bind, which is the same unbound receipt claim 50 left unmatched. The attempt flag is not written. Claim 83’s refusal to invent the route in order to clear `delivered` stays that refusal. The route exists for the receipt, not for the toggle.
