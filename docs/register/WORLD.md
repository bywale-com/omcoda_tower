## 0. Opening

### 0.1 Shape

| Layer | Shape in Tower V1 |
|---|---|
| **Acquisition** | **ALG** — one-tap Meta Approach yielding name + website + phone/email inside a click budget. Assisted/operator-led entry (**OLG**) is not deleted; it is a second real door into the same desk. |
| **Activation** | **Forward-deploy + agent-earned hard inputs.** A no-login prepared campaign is served first; database authorization and escrow acceptance are earned after readiness is legible. |
| **Application** | **Consultant-operated desk** over a private firm book. Client-side engagement is **automation-led once armed** — the consultant does not hand-send the loop. |
| **Evolution** | **Named, lightly held.** Running-firm friction becomes a documented gap, then a written affordance or backend facet, then regenerates into the product. Not a client lifecycle phase. |
| **Agent** | **Presentation feature** (request / respond / route). Not a seat, not a column, never fulfillment. |
| **Om Coda house (Operator)** | Lattice entity for acquisition/activation closure. **Not** a firm Board desk seat. |

### 0.2 Persona emergence law

> A Register persona exists only if omitting them leaves a **value-chain gap** — a capability or outcome with nowhere to land.

Consequences, held strictly:

- **Org-chart roles are delegation, not seats.** Firm admin, junior associate, virtual assistant, receptionist — same **Consultant** seat, differing later by permission features. They become a seat only when a capability cannot be attributed to the Consultant without breaking a chain. (Multi-consultant roles/permissions are an open Seed unknown — see residual 23.)
- **Operator is named for closure, not for need.** Without Om Coda operating the ads, the capture, the scrape, the templates, the prepared workspace and the two hard-input requests, the Consultant's acquisition and activation capabilities dangle into "the system". Naming Operator keeps those hops honest. It does **not** grant Om Coda a product desk.
- **The agent is not a seat.** It has no capability that is not a presentation of another entity's capability.
- **The end-client's household is not a seat.** Spouse/dependant facts live on the engagement record; Seed provides no household entity, so World provides none.

### 0.3 World sentence

> Tower is the always-on immigration-consultancy workspace that acquires a stranger consultant through a one-tap Meta Approach (name + website + channel within a click budget), activates by forward-deploying a no-login prepared campaign under their firm identity until they authorize database access and accept escrow, then runs the firm desk loop — keep the private contact book reachable, engage through opt-in → nudge → reactivation, refresh Client Data through touchpoints, re-evaluate service eligibility as facts and IRCC-shaped rules move, and campaign eligible people toward a booked meeting — without the consultant rechecking every file by hand, and without inventing public-before-contact detection of end-client eligibility.

### 0.5 Facet doctrine

1. **Activation sets the target; acquisition fulfills the seed quota.** Acquisition does not get to decide what it collects — activation's finish line decides, and acquisition's only job is to deliver that quota within its budget.
2. **Finish lines are input sets, not a vague yes.** Acquisition ends when name + website + phone/email land. Activation ends when database authorization and escrow acceptance land and the campaign can run.
3. **Click budget lives in acquisition only.** Nothing hard may be pulled backwards into it.
4. **Forward-deploy lives in activation.** Serving a prepared workspace is not acquisition work, no matter how much it looks like marketing.
5. **Entry surfaces are product surfaces.** Approach gets the same Register discipline as the desk (decomposition, admission, gap writing, affordance writing) — with the caveat that affordance writing on Approach is mostly persuasion texture, not desk inhabit.
6. **One Approach per acquired non-operator persona.** In V1 that is exactly one: the Consultant. Operator is never an acquired stranger; the Engagement contact is never acquired through Approach at all.
7. **Application does not reshape to make the growth bet cheaper.** If the ALG bet needs fewer inputs, that is a growth problem, not a licence to shrink the desk.
8. **The desk is the same desk either way.** ALG-provisioned and assisted-provisioned firms land in one application, one session matrix.

### 0.6 Terms — each defined once, plain language

| Term | Plain meaning (used consistently below) |
|---|---|
| **Facet** | One of four layers a persona can be served in: acquisition, activation, application, evolution. |
| **Acquisition** | Getting the cheap inputs needed to *provision* something for a stranger, autonomously, inside a click budget. Ends when those inputs land. |
| **Activation** | The systemized path from provisioned stranger to the last inputs required to *run*. Ends when the campaign can run. |
| **Application** | The desk performing the core outcome once running. |
| **Evolution** | Product change driven by running-firm feedback through Register passes. |
| **Running** | State after the last activation input: the campaign performs without asking for more required activation inputs. |
| **ALG (agent-led growth)** | Cheap public inputs are given; the system extrapolates and prepares; an agent earns the hard inputs mid-activation. No sales call as the primary door, no long self-serve onboarding form. |
| **Assisted / operator-led entry** | A human at Om Coda collects the inputs (call, assisted onboarding, seeded provision). Still a real path in V1. |
| **Self-serve entry** | The persona fills the forms themselves. Not the Tower V1 bet. |
| **Approach** | The acquisition strip of surfaces: feed context → ad → capture → continue your scroll. |
| **Seed inputs** | Firm/consultant name, website, and one channel (phone **or** email). |
| **Click budget** | The deliberate cap on stranger effort during acquisition. Forced at one click as a design constraint; relaxed only as a finding, because clicks are easier to add than to remove. |
| **Forward-deploy** | After seed inputs land: read public firm facts, instantiate Om Coda's own campaign templates, and stage a working campaign under the firm's identity — before the firm has given anything hard. |
| **Prepared workspace** | The no-login artifact that forward-deploy produces. Proves **readiness**, not value. |
| **Hard inputs** | The two things that cannot be inferred: authorization into the firm's database (or the equivalent that makes the campaign real), and acceptance of payment terms. |
| **Escrow** | Contingent commercial terms between the **firm and Om Coda** — cost is zero or held until outcome terms are met. Tower does not hold immigrant settlement funds. |
| **Contingent cost** | Om Coda's money posture: the buyer's cost is held until outcome terms are met. |
| **Public-detection products** | A different class of product where one cheap public fact (a legal business name, a parcel identifier) is already enough to run real detection. Tower is **not** one of these: real end-client eligibility needs the firm's own book. This is why Approach must not ask for a database inside the click budget. |
| **Audit** | A reachability gate over a set of contacts. One question only: can we reach this contact, and can we start a sequence on them? Verdicts: reachable / partial / unreachable. Not scoring, not forecasting, not a pass/fail sales ceremony, not a minimum-contact-count gate. |
| **Agent sequence** | An ordered set of channel + copy steps. Launch and reactivation agents are composites of those steps. Composing them is configuration; sending them is automation. |
| **Automation workflow** | Trigger (pull scoped records) → conditions / rules evaluate → structured output → downstream actions or enrollment. |
| **Engagement phases** | Opt-in (consent, first channel open) → re-engagement nudges (ongoing touchpoints that collect and refresh facts) → reactivation (re-engaging quiet contacts when rules or data warrant). |
| **Armed vs active** | Armed = template ready, nothing sending (firm-side ghost). Active = the sequence is executing. |
| **Silenced** | Opted out or otherwise excluded from automatic outreach; also the state of a contact who never opted in. |
| **Nudge** | A touchpoint that carries a data ask. Nudges are the data-collection mechanism, not spam. |
| **Signal vs motion** | Every detection — including a missing fact — is recorded as a **signal** when found. Whether anything is *sent* is a separate **motion** decision under fixed precedence. |
| **Engine 2 precedence** | Reactivation first, always, if anything is reactivation-worthy. Nudge only when nothing is. One nudge consolidates every outstanding self-reportable need into a single form. When reactivation fires, other signals ride along as agenda on the consultant's pre-meeting brief — never as parallel outreach. **One client, one motion, ever.** |
| **Self-reportable** | A fact the client knows offhand (a score, a date, a status). Eligible for forms and pre-meeting capture. |
| **Document-dependent** | A fact requiring paperwork (credential assessments, labour-market paperwork, employer revenue). **Never** enters a form; becomes a tracked work item. |
| **Employer-directed** | The ask is aimed at the firm, not the client. Routed separately. |
| **Service eligibility** | What the firm can *sell* this person now, computed from firm-held plus engagement-collected facts against shipped or firm-configured rules. Pathway pass = **any** pathway assertion, not one favoured pathway. |
| **Reference tables** | Versioned immigration data (categories, trades, cutoffs, provincial identifiers) that Tower maintains as data — updated without a code deploy. |
| **Analysis** | The human-readable read-out of an evaluation: headlines, deltas, service candidates, nudge/reactivation hints. Machine output retained underneath for power users. |
| **Live brief** | The consultant's pre-meeting view. Re-computed on write-back, not a snapshot. |
| **Loop-closer** | The behaviour at meeting-booked: outstanding self-reportable fields become a capture form pending the meeting; any write-back re-evaluates immediately. |

## 1. Value-chain seats

### 1.1 Consultant (firm operator) — desk seat, acquired persona

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why they exist** | Omit them and there is no stranger to prepare a workspace for, no one to authorize a database, no one to accept terms, no one to arm audits/sequences/automations, no one whose book is being worked, and no one to take the booked meeting. Every other hop in the lattice terminates on them or starts from them. |
| **Served how — acquisition** | Meta feed context → ad whose only job is to make one-tap output legible → capture of name + website + phone/email → **continue your scroll**. Pre-framed that an agent will follow and prepare, so the first message does not read as spam. No thank-you dashboard; the agent comes to them. |
| **Served how — activation** | A no-login prepared workspace staged under their firm identity, built from public firm facts plus Om Coda's own launch → re-engagement → reactivation methodology. An agent walks beside them with narration and deep links, positioned as *"I prepared this campaign"* — not *"look at our platform."* Then two requests: authorize the database, accept escrow. Unbudgeted: effort here is allowed to be non-trivial because the agent is present. |
| **Served how — application** | Passwordless sign-in into a firm-scoped workspace: **Board** (client list, phase signals, entry to a client), **Client Data** (profile, Q&A, forms, eligibility-relevant facts), **Activity / journey** (timeline of touchpoints, escalations, armed vs active), **Contacts** (directory, indicators, import), **Hub** (Audits, Agents, Automations and related workspace tabs). |
| **Served how — assisted door** | Firm and users provisioned through versioned seed manifests, then one-time-code sign-in into the same desk. Assisted onboarding can also supply the activation inputs. |
| **Purpose they serve** | So that an **Engagement contact** can be reached on a working channel, asked once for what they know, judged on current facts and current rules, left alone when they ask, and invited to a meeting that is worth their time. |
| **Primary object, by facet** | Approach session (acquisition) → Prepared workspace (activation) → the firm's book of Engagement contacts plus Hub configuration (application). |
| **Admit iff** | **Approach:** a stranger in a Meta feed who can complete seed capture. **Activation:** seed inputs landed and a prepared workspace served. **Desk:** a provisioned user row under a firm tenancy with a valid session — reached either post-activation or through assisted provision. |
| **Never see** | Another firm's book. A "create your firm from nothing" self-serve path presented as the core door. Client-private channel content the firm was never admitted to. Om Coda's internal Register tooling as product chrome. Credits or a sales call offered as equal doors at the activation payment moment. |
| **Natural needs — acquisition** | Understand what one tap yields. Give three cheap facts without a form. Be able to disbelieve cheaply and keep scrolling. Not be persuaded at. Not be asked for a database. |
| **Natural needs — activation** | See something real before giving anything hard. Check the claim without logging in. Understand that firm branding on the workspace is identity, not a claim that Om Coda already holds their clients. Authorize a database on their own terms. Face one money door with held cost. Not be asked for client personal data before the database step. |
| **Natural needs — application** | Land on a Board that looks like their own book. See phase and eligibility signals without opening every file. Bring contacts in. Know who is reachable. Author sequences that sound like the firm. Let automations re-evaluate. Read *why* someone is eligible in language, not machine output. See who is sequenced, silenced, unsequenced. Arrive at a meeting already briefed. Trust that no client is getting two motions at once. |
| **Not a persona** | Firm admin, junior associate, virtual assistant, office manager — the same seat, plus future permission features. |
| **Interest** | Booked consults and retainer starts are leaking out of a list that sits dark. Time is going into manually rechecking files as draws, permits, language results, work history and provincial facts move. Reputation is at risk from uninformed outreach. |
| **Friction** | Distrust of an outsized claim in a feed. Fear of committing data or money before proof. Zero tolerance for click cost. A book so dirty they cannot start sequences at all. |
| **Build honesty** | The desk exists in prototype shape: journeys and UI are there; runtime is largely mock or session-scoped; workflow, run and constants edits do not survive refresh; contact import opens but does not fully mutate the living list; operations and actions modules are stubs; event triggers are thinner than manual ones; one-time-code sign-in is real for provisioned users. **Approach, forward-deploy and the escrow door are a bet, not a build.** |

### 1.2 Engagement contact (Client) — desk seat, touchpoint-only

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why they exist** | Omit them and there is no eligibility subject, no fact to refresh, no meeting to book. The Consultant's entire application chain has nowhere to land, and the core outcome cannot close. |
| **Served how** | Firm-branded touchpoints only — email, text, forms, question-and-answer — delivered when their state allows outreach. Not an operator console. Not a mirror of the firm's workspace. |
| **Purpose they serve** | So that the **Consultant** can hold a current picture of their situation, offer a service that is actually available now, and close a booked meeting. |
| **Primary object** | Their own engagement record, and the touchpoints currently addressed to them. Nothing else. |
| **Admit iff** | Present in the firm's book **and** in a state that permits outreach — not silenced, past the reachability gate where the intended channel requires it, and enrolled by a sequence or automation. The firm must already be **running**; Approach never admits end-clients. |
| **Never see** | The firm's Board. The Hub's Agents or Automations editors. Any other contact. Firm-wide audit results. Another row's Analysis. Om Coda's internal Register tooling. The Approach strip or the prepared-workspace activation chrome. Escrow or any firm money surface. |
| **Natural needs** | Receive a consent request before deep collection. Understand *why this firm is messaging me*. Be asked once, for things they actually know, rather than field by field. Not be asked to produce paperwork through a form field. Be able to go quiet. Pay nothing. Be invited to a meeting only when there is a reason. |
| **Not a persona** | A spouse or dependant on the same immigration file. Their facts live on the record; Seed provides no household entity. |
| **Desk depth ceiling** | **Touchpoint-only.** Whether this ever becomes an authenticated client portal is an open Seed unknown. Do not plant a client console that mirrors the consultant's information architecture. |
| **Interest** | Timely advice at the moment their situation or the rules changed, from a firm that already knows them. |
| **Friction** | Outreach that feels like spam. Being asked repeatedly. Being asked for documents in a web form. Not being able to stop it. |
| **Facets** | **No Approach** — one Approach per acquired non-operator persona, and that is the Consultant. **No activation inputs** — they are never the payer. They appear only in **application**, only once the firm is running. |
| **Money** | Detection and engagement are free to them. They never transact with Om Coda. |

### 1.3 Operator (Om Coda house) — lattice entity, **not** a desk seat

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why they exist** | Omit them and the Consultant's acquisition and activation capabilities have no counterparty: no one serves the ad, holds the click budget, persists the capture, reads the public firm facts, instantiates the templates, serves the prepared workspace, presents the walkthrough, requests the two hard inputs, holds the terms, or flips the campaign to running. Those hops would dangle into "the system" — which is exactly the failure this law exists to prevent. |
| **Served how** | Om Coda runs the Approach surfaces and instruments them; persists captures and attributes sessions; reads public firm facts; instantiates its own methodology templates; serves the prepared workspace; presents the agent walkthrough; requests database authorization and escrow acceptance; stores the authorization, holds escrow, and flips the campaign to running; and operates the assisted door (seeded provision plus one-time-code sign-in) when ALG is not the path. |
| **Purpose they serve** | So that the **Consultant** can be acquired inside a click budget and carried to running without a sales call as a peer door, without prepayment, and without the application desk being shrunk to make the growth bet easier. |
| **Primary object** | Approach surfaces and the Prepared workspace. **Not** the firm's engagement book as operator-of-record. |
| **Admit iff** | An Om Coda house role on Approach or activation tooling. **Never** as a login on a firm's product desk. |
| **Never see / never do** | Act as the firm's consultant inside Board or Hub as though Om Coda were the client desk. Appear as a third persona in the firm's own product. Take end-client outreach decisions that belong to the firm. |
| **Natural needs** | Instrument the two acquisition failure modes separately: *did not understand* versus *understood and did not tap*. Treat continue-scroll as data rather than loss. Earn the hard inputs in the middle of activation rather than at the front. Keep escrow the only door at the payment moment. Hand off to a desk they did not have to redesign. |
| **Not a persona (desk)** | Not a consultant desk seat, and not a Register product-user seat for immigration firms. Lattice entity only. |
| **Facets** | Owns the **supply side** of acquisition (Approach) and activation (forward-deploy, hard-input requests, terms). Does **not** own application as a desk user. Feeds **evolution** by turning running-firm friction into documented gaps. |
| **Boundary residual** | Om Coda also keeps immigration reference data current as versioned data, which lands inside application. World records this as **house supply**, not as promotion of Operator to an application desk seat. Who owns rule packs — and who carries the liability — is open (residual 19). |

## 3. Primary objects and admission matrices

### 3.1 Object inventory

| Object | Role in World | Has an admission matrix here |
|---|---|---|
| **Firm** | Tenancy; owns users and the book | Via firm session |
| **Consultant (user)** | Firm operator; the acquired persona | It is a seat, not an object |
| **Engagement contact / Client** | Shared book object; touchpoint subject; eligibility subject | **Yes** (§3.2) |
| **Approach surfaces** | The acquisition strip | **Yes** (§3.3) |
| **Prepared workspace** | The activation artifact | **Yes** (§3.4) |
| **Firm session** | Desk access, both doors | **Yes** (§3.5) |
| **Audit batch** | Reachability gate over a set | Secondary — named by Consultant capability |
| **Agent sequence** | Ordered channel + copy composite | Secondary |
| **Automation workflow** | Trigger → evaluate → enroll/act | Secondary |
| **Immigration reference tables** | Versioned rule inputs | Secondary; house-maintained |
| **Signal / motion** | Detection record versus outreach decision | Not an object with states in V1 — it is the law that governs contact-state transitions |

**Cell notation, all matrices.** **V** = the persona may see the object in this state. **—** = not in view; showing it is a world bug. **T** = the persona owns a named transition out of or into this state. **V · T** = both.

### 3.2 Engagement contact (application)

| State | Meaning | Consultant | Engagement contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| `imported` | In the firm's book; not yet sequenced, no active consent | V · T *import / add* | — |
| `audit_blocked` | Fails reachability for the intended channel | V · T *fix / exclude* | — |
| `opt_in` | Consent launch active or first consent pending | V · T *arm / stop* | V · T *consent / ignore* |
| `re_engagement` | Consented; nudge cycles collecting and refreshing facts | V | V · T *reply / submit form* |
| `reactivation_armed` | Quiet or stale; criteria met; template ready, nothing sending | V | — |
| `reactivation_active` | Reactivation sequence executing | V | V · T *reply / submit form* |
| `eligible` | Rules and Analysis surfaced service eligibility | V | — *(firm-side unless a touchpoint carries it)* |
| `meeting_booked` | Core outcome closed for this cycle | V | V · T *book* (when an invitation exists) |
| `silenced` | Opted out or excluded from automatic outreach | V · T *mark / clear* | V · T *silence* |

**Happy spine.** `imported` → (reachability clear) → `opt_in` → `re_engagement` → `eligible` may assert at any point once facts exist → `reactivation_armed` / `reactivation_active` as warranted → `meeting_booked`.

**Branches.** Any state → `silenced`. `imported` → `audit_blocked` until fixed or excluded.

**Laws the matrix encodes.**

- `eligible` is a **signal**, not a motion. Reaching it does not authorize a send; Engine 2 precedence decides that separately.
- `reactivation_armed` is deliberately client-invisible. Armed means ready, not sent.
- Whether `eligible` and `reactivation_armed` coexist with a nudge is decided by precedence, never by whichever module noticed first.
- `meeting_booked` closes a **cycle**, not the relationship: facts and rules keep moving and the contact re-enters the loop.
- Two motions on one contact at once is a world bug regardless of what the matrix permits row by row.

### 3.3 Approach surfaces (acquisition) — Consultant | Operator only

| State | Meaning | Consultant | Operator |
|---|---|---|---|
| `in_feed` | Scrolling Meta; Approach context available | V | V · T *serve ad* |
| `ad_legible` | One-tap output is understood — or instrumented as understood | V | V |
| `seed_captured` | Name + website + phone/email landed — **acquisition ends** | V · T *give seed inputs* | V · T *receive, persist, attribute* |
| `continued_scroll` | Did not tap, or left — cheap disbelief | V · T *leave* | V · T *instrument* |

**Spine.** `in_feed` → `ad_legible` → `seed_captured` → handoff into the prepared workspace. **Branch.** `in_feed` or `ad_legible` → `continued_scroll`.

**Pins.** The click budget applies through `seed_captured` and nowhere else. Database authorization and escrow are **not** Approach states and must never be added as ones. `continued_scroll` is a legitimate terminal state producing data, not a funnel leak to be plugged with persuasion.

**Engagement contact: never a column here.** Approach acquires the firm, not the firm's clients.

### 3.4 Prepared workspace (activation) — Consultant | Operator only

| State | Meaning | Consultant | Operator |
|---|---|---|---|
| `forward_deployed` | No-login prepared campaign under the firm's identity; walkthrough available | V · T *walk / inspect* | V · T *deploy / narrate* |
| `db_auth_pending` | Hard input requested: authorize the firm database, or the equivalent that makes the campaign real | V · T *authorize* | V · T *request through the agent* |
| `escrow_pending` | Hard input requested: accept contingent terms | V · T *accept* | V · T *present — one door only* |
| `running` | Last activation input landed; the campaign can perform — **activation ends** | V · T *enter the desk* | V |
| `abandoned` | Left before running | V · T *leave* | V · T *instrument* |

**Spine.** `forward_deployed` → `db_auth_pending` and `escrow_pending` (order may interleave) → `running`. **Branch.** Any state → `abandoned`.

**Pins.** Forward-deploy is activation work. The firm's identity on the workspace is branding and authentication detail — never a claim that Om Coda already holds their clients. The workspace proves **readiness**; value is proved by escrow plus a real run. Marginal cost is a public read plus compute — the agent presents, automation executes. Prepaid credits and a sales call are **not** peer cells at `escrow_pending`.

**Engagement contact: never a column here.** No client PII is required to reach `running`.

### 3.5 Firm session (application, both doors)

| State | Meaning | Consultant |
|---|---|---|
| `unprovisioned` | Email is not a known user — generic success response, no code sent (so the surface cannot be used to enumerate who is a customer) | — |
| `provisioned_signed_out` | User exists; no valid session | V · T *request and verify a one-time code* |
| `provisioned_signed_in` | Valid session cookie | V · T *sign out* |

**Join note.** The ALG path provisions the user as part of activation reaching `running`. The assisted path provisions through versioned seed manifests. Same matrix, same desk, either way. Om Coda's internal Register gate is internal tooling and is not product chrome for firms.

**Engagement contact and Operator: never columns here.**

## 4. Gate versus decision ritual

### 4.1 Hard human gates (what genuinely requires a person)

| Gate | Layer | Who acts | Why it cannot be automated away |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed capture — name, website, channel | Acquisition | Consultant | A stranger must act; cheap by design |
| Firm and user provision (assisted door) | Application bootstrap | Operator | Tenancy is intentional, not self-minted |
| One-time-code verification | Application | Consultant | Proves control of the email |
| Database authorization | Activation | Consultant | A high-trust act over the firm's own book |
| Escrow acceptance | Activation | Consultant | Money and terms |
| Client consent / not silenced | Application | Engagement contact | Ethics and law |
| Meeting booking | Application | Engagement contact | Human commitment — and the core success event |
| Refusing illegal or unethical outreach | Application | Consultant | Firm responsibility, not platform policy |

**Not Tower gates** (they belong to other products' worlds): power of attorney before an officer, finder licensure, disbursement of public funds.

### 4.2 Gate versus decision chrome — bug and fix

A **gate** is a hard human act that must happen. A **decision** is a rendering choice about what a persona sees. Confusing the two produces these bugs:

| Bug | Fix |
|---|---|
| Showing "eligible" as client-facing chrome on a touchpoint that only asked for one form field | Eligibility is Consultant and automation view. Seed adds no client-facing eligibility notice — do not invent one |
| Showing Board phase icons to a client | Clients never see the Board |
| Treating `reactivation_armed` as client-visible | Armed is a firm-side ghost. Clients admit at `reactivation_active`, or on a sent touchpoint |
| Implying a client can read Automations Analysis | Matrix cell is **—** |
| Showing a client any Approach, prepared-workspace or escrow surface | Engagement contact never admits on those objects at all |
| Presenting the reachability gate as a pass/fail score, a forecast, or a minimum-contact threshold | Audit asks one operational question: can we reach them and start a sequence |
| Presenting an external assessment narrative as the in-app reachability product | Sales collateral may sell trust; the in-app surface stays operational |
| Firing one nudge per missing field | One motion, one consolidated form, every outstanding self-reportable need |
| Sending a nudge while something is reactivation-worthy | Reactivation outranks nudge, always. Secondary signals become brief agenda |
| Showing a snapshot brief at the meeting | The brief is live; write-back re-evaluates immediately |
| Offering credits or a sales call beside escrow at the payment moment | Escrow is the only door at that moment |
| Asking for a database or a payment inside the Approach strip | Both are activation, both are outside the click budget |

### 4.3 The ritual (run before any persona UI)

1. Name the object in play and the persona in the seat.
2. Name the state. If you cannot name it, the state is missing from World — write it here first.
3. Walk the matrix row. Anything on screen that the row marks **—** is a world bug, not a design preference.
4. Check ownership: is this persona actually the **T** for the action being offered, or are you offering someone else's transition?
5. Check the motion law before rendering any outreach affordance: is there already a motion on this contact?
6. Check the facet: is this surface asking for something that belongs to a different layer (a hard input inside acquisition, a click budget inside activation)?
7. Only then design. Fix World before planting anything downstream.

## 5. Never invent — plain language

Each of these is a world bug. They come straight from Seed's hard stops.

1. **Detecting an end-client's immigration eligibility from public sources before anyone has contacted them, as core function.** That is a different class of product — one where a single cheap public fact is already enough to run detection. 2. **An agent seat, or an agent column in any admission matrix.** The agent is a presentation feature and has no capabilities of its own.

## 7. Agent — feature, not a seat

The conversational or text agent, and the Hub's sequence composer, are **presentation and configuration** over verbs that belong elsewhere. The agent requests, responds and routes. It never fulfils.

| The agent does this (presentation) | This is **not** the agent — it is automation and wiring |
|---|---|
| Asks for name, website, phone or email on Approach | Persists the capture and attributes the session |
| Explains what the prepared workspace already shows | Forward-deploys the workspace; reads public firm facts; instantiates methodology templates |
| Asks the consultant to authorize the database and accept terms | Stores the authorization, holds the terms, flips the campaign to running |
| Points at "arm this reachability check" or "send this sequence" in the Hub | Runs the reachability pull, enrols contacts, sends messages on schedule |
| Answers "why is this person eligible?" by routing to Analysis | Evaluates immigration rules against Client Data |
| Presents a consolidated form to a client | Decides that a nudge may fire at all, under precedence |
| Narrates what the brief says | Re-evaluates on write-back and keeps the brief live |
| — | Sends one-time codes, issues session cookies, moves money |

**Consequences held in this document.**

- The agent has **no admission column** in §3. It cannot, because it has no state of its own to be admitted to.
- Every capability it appears to have is an Operator or Consultant capability being presented.
- Composing a sequence is configuration; sending it is automation. Both may appear in the same Hub, and they are not the same act.
- Never label the automation runtime or the authentication service as "the agent doing work." Doing so is how a presentation feature quietly becomes an unaccountable seat.

## Underlying assumptions pass (BA)

### A. Ecosystem / product (general) — unspoken assumptions

**1. There are enough immigration consultancies in the target geography to make a paid acquisition channel worth building at all.** — *Why worth considering:* A few hundred targetable firms behaves like a sales list, not an ad market, and would flip the whole growth bet back to operator-led.

**2. Contact details in a dormant firm book survive the years of dormancy well enough that reachability is meaningfully above zero.** — *Why worth considering:* The docs treat a dirty list as a teachable byproduct; if decay is the norm rather than the exception, reachability is the product's real ceiling.

**3. Immigration rules move often enough to keep producing re-evaluation events, but not so fast that shipped rule packs are wrong most of the time.** — *Why worth considering:* Retention rests on rule movement generating fresh signals; too little movement and the loop goes quiet after the first sweep, too much and the packs are a liability.

**4. A contact's eligibility can change materially without the firm finding out through ordinary means.** — *Why worth considering:* If people simply call their consultant when their situation changes, always-on detection is solving a problem the phone already solves.

**5. Dormant contacts have not already retained another consultant.** — *Why worth considering:* The addressable value of a book assumes the relationship is re-openable rather than closed by a competitor.

**6. Firms have spare capacity to take the extra meetings — the bottleneck is lead flow, not consultant hours.** — *Why worth considering:* If the desk is already full, more booked meetings are a cost, not a win, and the core success metric stops being desirable.

**7. A booked meeting converts to paid work often enough that contingent terms can be priced against it.** — *Why worth considering:* Escrow tied to an outcome requires the outcome to carry money; a cheap meeting makes the release term unfundable.

**8. Asserting what a firm can sell someone is commercial triage, not the practice of immigration consulting.** — *Why worth considering:* If regulators read a service-eligibility assertion as advice, the rule packs become a licensing question rather than a data question.

**9. Professional regulators do not restrict automated or bulk solicitation by licensed consultants.** — *Why worth considering:* Sequences firing under a firm's name are a marketing act by a regulated professional; a conduct rule against it would gate the whole loop.

**10. Eligibility is computable from a bounded, mostly deterministic fact set.** — *Why worth considering:* The signal/motion law assumes a rule engine can reach a defensible verdict; if real files turn on discretion and edge judgement, Analysis becomes a suggestion box.

**11. Self-reported facts are accurate enough to base a service pitch on.** — *Why worth considering:* Scores, dates and work years drive pathway verdicts; systematic optimism in self-report produces confident wrong eligibility at scale.

**12. A wrong eligibility read is recoverable because the consultant catches it in the meeting.** — *Why worth considering:* This is the safety valve that makes shipping imperfect rules acceptable, and nothing in the flow enforces that the check actually happens.

**13. Email and text at book-scale will actually be delivered.** — *Why worth considering:* Domain reputation, carrier filtering and re-engaging years-cold addresses are exactly the conditions that trigger spam handling; the loop assumes messages land.

**14. Either the firm's own sending identity is technically available, or a substitute can carry the firm-branded promise.** — *Why worth considering:* "Firm → client value-add" depends on the message looking like it came from the firm; the sending-identity mechanics are never named.

**15. The market pays software prices for this, not a share of the work it generates.** — *Why worth considering:* Escrow-on-outcome edges toward performance pricing; if firms expect a commission model instead, packaging and margin change shape.

**16. Firms will trust an unknown vendor with their entire client book.** — *Why worth considering:* The single hardest input in the model is a data handover, and vendor credibility is treated as something the prepared workspace supplies rather than something the market withholds.

**17. A breach of a firm's immigration contact book would be survivable rather than terminal.** — *Why worth considering:* This population's data is unusually sensitive; the docs never place security posture among the load-bearing conditions.

**18. Whatever infrastructure holds the data satisfies the privacy and residency expectations of firms and their regulators.** — *Why worth considering:* Canadian professional buyers commonly ask where the data sits before authorizing anything; the answer is assumed.

**19. Firms can be processed as a third party under the privacy consent their clients originally gave.** — *Why worth considering:* Processing an old file through a new vendor is a distinct legal step from messaging that person, and only the messaging side gets flagged.

**20. If any exemption is doing the work for re-contacting past clients, it survives multi-year dormancy.** — *Why worth considering:* Existing-relationship allowances typically expire; "re-engage dormant contacts" may be the one motion the exemption stops covering.

**21. Many end-contacts sit outside Canada, so Canadian consent rules may not be the only or governing regime.** — *Why worth considering:* Consent is flagged as an open question but framed domestically; an overseas prospect base makes it a multi-jurisdiction problem instead.

**22. English-only client-facing copy is sufficient for a Canada-shaped V1.** — *Why worth considering:* Quebec-facing work and a multilingual applicant base both push toward French and other languages, which changes sequence authoring, not just translation.

**23. One product shape serves a solo practice and a twenty-consultant firm without forking.** — *Why worth considering:* Roles are deferred as an unknown, but the *pricing, book size and workflow* differences between those two buyers may be bigger than a permission feature.

**24. Firms will not require message-by-message approval before outreach goes out.** — *Why worth considering:* A professional carrying personal liability may insist on reviewing every send, which quietly converts always-on into an approval queue.

**25. Firms will let Tower become the outreach system of record even while their CRM stays the client system of record.** — *Why worth considering:* One-way intake means two systems both believe they own the relationship; someone has to accept living with the drift.

**26. Identity resolution across a messy book is tractable enough to avoid messaging the same person twice.** — *Why worth considering:* "One client, one motion" is enforceable only if the system can tell that two rows are one human.

**27. The same person does not appear in two firms' books inside Tower in a way anyone notices.** — *Why worth considering:* Overlapping books are normal in a referral-heavy market, and two firms nudging the same contact is a trust event the model has no place for.

**28. There is enough unworked demand inside a typical book that engaging it does not just pull forward work the firm would have won anyway.** — *Why worth considering:* If the loop mostly cannibalizes existing pipeline, the firm sees activity rather than incremental revenue.

**29. Value persists past the first full sweep of the book.** — *Why worth considering:* The first pass yields a burst; ongoing money depends on rule and fact movement producing a steady trickle, which is asserted rather than sized.

**30. Booking infrastructure exists on the firm side and can be connected.** — *Why worth considering:* The core success event is a booked meeting, and nothing in the model names how a calendar gets wired or who owns the slot.

**31. Attribution of a booked meeting to Tower is uncontested.** — *Why worth considering:* Outcome-contingent money needs an agreed answer to "did you cause this?", and firms have every incentive to argue otherwise.

**32. Incumbent CRMs and marketing tools will not add a re-evaluation loop and commoditize the wedge.** — *Why worth considering:* The differentiator is rule depth plus re-crunch; both are features an incumbent with distribution could bolt on.

**33. A rival will not reach running immigration firms first with a similar loop.** — *Why worth considering:* The bet is validated slowly through Register passes; the market position assumes time to do that.

**34. Contacts do not change country, status or life stage faster than nudges can refresh the record.** — *Why worth considering:* If the record decays faster than the cadence refreshes it, eligibility is computed on stale facts most of the time.

**35. Immigration timelines are long enough that a multi-year drip relationship is normal rather than strange.** — *Why worth considering:* The whole engagement philosophy assumes patience on both sides of a relationship with no near-term transaction.

**36. Eligibility that depends on a spouse or dependant can be resolved through the primary contact.** — *Why worth considering:* Household facts drive real pathway outcomes and the docs explicitly decline to model a household, so proxy reporting is silently doing the work.

**37. Firms will import in bulk rather than trickle contacts in.** — *Why worth considering:* Audit, enrolment and the "your book looks like your book" landing all assume the book arrives at once.

**38. A typical book is large enough that automation beats a person with a spreadsheet.** — *Why worth considering:* At a few hundred contacts, a cheap assistant is a credible substitute, which changes who the real competitor is.

**39. Per-contact messaging and compute cost stays small relative to a booked meeting.** — *Why worth considering:* Always-on over a whole book multiplies a small unit cost by a large number, and no cost model is stated.

**40. Firms will accept a vendor's rule pack over their own judgement, at least for triage.** — *Why worth considering:* Professionals are trained to distrust outside determinations; if they insist on configuring everything, the shipped-pack advantage disappears.

**41. Reference data can be watched and maintained continuously by a small house function.** — *Why worth considering:* Versioned tables updated without a deploy is an operational promise, not just an architecture choice, and the operational side is uncosted.

**42. The published sources for that reference data are stable and structured enough to track cheaply.** — *Why worth considering:* If the inputs are PDFs and news posts, currency becomes ongoing manual labour with an error rate.

**8. They read the language the touchpoint is written in well enough to answer.** — *Why worth considering:* Language ability is literally one of the facts being assessed, so low proficiency is a population feature, not an edge case.

**9. They will trust an automated firm-branded message in a market saturated with immigration fraud.** — *Why worth considering:* This population is heavily targeted by scams, so the trust bar for an unexpected message about their status is unusually high.

**10. They will not be unsettled that the firm still holds their old details and knows their situation.** — *Why worth considering:* The same message reads as attentive service or as surveillance depending on how the relationship ended.

**11. They have not retained another consultant in the interim.** — *Why worth considering:* Being re-approached by an old firm mid-engagement elsewhere is an awkward event with no handling in the model.

**12. They accept a system rather than their consultant re-opening the conversation.** — *Why worth considering:* The relationship was originally human; the resumption is not, and that shift is never acknowledged.

**13. They will complete a consolidated form rather than abandon a long one.** — *Why worth considering:* One-form consolidation is chosen to avoid nudge spam, and it trades that risk for abandonment risk that goes unmeasured.

**14. They will answer without any incentive.** — *Why worth considering:* The value exchange is future advice; the effort is now, and free-to-the-contact is treated as sufficient motivation.

**15. They can answer the self-reportable questions without digging out paperwork.** — *Why worth considering:* The forms-versus-tracked-work split assumes a clean line between remembered and documented facts, and dates and scores often sit on the wrong side of it.

**16. They act as an individual decision-maker.** — *Why worth considering:* Immigration decisions are usually household decisions, and a single contact record cannot carry a joint deliberation.

**17. They can and will report a spouse's or dependant's facts on request.** — *Why worth considering:* Several real pathway outcomes need those facts, and the docs explicitly refuse a household entity.

**18. They will not share the form link with others.** — *Why worth considering:* An unauthenticated capture link circulating in a community group contaminates the record and the eligibility read.

**19. Their going quiet reliably means disinterest.** — *Why worth considering:* Reactivation criteria treat silence as a state; a spam folder, a full inbox or a changed number produce the same silence with the opposite meaning.

**20. They can find and use the way to stop the messages.** — *Why worth considering:* Silenced is a state the model relies on, and a person who cannot opt out escalates instead.

**21. They will not escalate to a regulator or a platform when annoyed.** — *Why worth considering:* One complaint against a regulated professional costs the firm far more than the campaign earned.

**22. A meeting is a low enough commitment that they will take it.** — *Why worth considering:* The core success event assumes talking is free and easy; a paid or formal consultation is a very different conversion.

**23. Booked implies attended often enough for the metric to mean something.** — *Why worth considering:* The product measures booking, the firm values attendance, and no-show rates in cold-reactivated audiences are typically high.

**24. They can afford immigration services if eligible.** — *Why worth considering:* Eligibility and purchasing ability are separate; the loop optimizes only the first.

**25. Their circumstances change on a rhythm the nudge cadence can track.** — *Why worth considering:* Cadence is a design choice while life events are not, and a mismatch means either stale data or over-messaging.

**26. Facts reported at capture are still true at the meeting.** — *Why worth considering:* The brief is live with respect to write-back, not with respect to the world; nothing re-asks.

**27. They will stay tolerant of periodic messages over years.** — *Why worth considering:* Fatigue accumulates across the relationship even when each individual touch is well-behaved.

**28. They are not receiving similar outreach from several other firms.** — *Why worth considering:* If re-engagement becomes common practice, the differentiator is inbox competition rather than rule depth.

**29. The one channel the firm holds is the one they actually use.** — *Why worth considering:* Reachability is judged per channel, and an old work email passes validation while reaching nobody.

**30. They will click a link from a message on a phone.** — *Why worth considering:* Forms and capture pages assume a device, a browser and a willingness to leave the message.

**31. Being told what they might qualify for reads as helpful rather than as false hope or pressure.** — *Why worth considering:* The framing is what makes the value-add legitimate; misread, it becomes exactly the uninformed outreach the firm fears.

**32. They will not ask who the third party is.** — *Why worth considering:* Firm-branded delivery only holds if the vendor stays invisible, and any disclosure requirement breaks that.

**33. Their original consent, or the relationship itself, covers this new kind of contact.** — *Why worth considering:* Consent for a past file is narrower than consent for ongoing marketing, and the difference lands on the firm.

**34. A silenced contact staying silenced forever is acceptable.** — *Why worth considering:* Re-consent is unspecified, so the practical outcome is permanent loss of anyone who ever paused the relationship.

**35. Urgency in their own timeline — an expiry, a draw, a status change — makes them responsive.** — *Why worth considering:* This is the implicit hook that makes nudges welcome; without it every touchpoint is unprompted marketing.

**36. They do not need identity verification before their facts are trusted.** — *Why worth considering:* Service eligibility is asserted from unverified self-report attached to an unauthenticated touchpoint.

**37. They will not treat the firm's re-engagement as a claim of ongoing representation.** — *Why worth considering:* A message about eligibility from a former consultant can reasonably be read as continued professional responsibility.

**38. Their answers can be trusted as coming from them.** — *Why worth considering:* Family members, agents and community helpers frequently answer on someone's behalf in this domain.

**39. They will not expect a reply on their own terms.** — *Why worth considering:* Automated outreach invites inbound questions, and touchpoint-only gives them nowhere to land.

**40. Nothing in their situation makes contact itself risky for them.** — *Why worth considering:* For some statuses, an unsolicited message about immigration standing arriving on a shared device is a real-world harm the model never weighs.

### D. Operator (Om Coda house) — unspoken assumptions

**1. Om Coda can fund a paid acquisition test before any revenue arrives.** — *Why worth considering:* Contingent cost pushes revenue behind an outcome, so ad spend and forward-deploy compute are financed entirely out of pocket.

**2. Om Coda can survive the cash-flow shape that contingent terms create.** — *Why worth considering:* Held cost is a buyer benefit and a seller burden; the burden side is never sized.

**3. Om Coda can obtain and keep an advertising account in good standing for immigration-adjacent claims.** — *Why worth considering:* Platform review is named as a risk to the ad, not as a risk to the account, and account loss removes the channel entirely.

**4. Legibility is achievable inside the constraints of a feed ad unit.** — *Why worth considering:* "Never persuade, only make output legible" is a hard craft constraint in a format that is a few seconds of a scroll.

**5. Om Coda can attribute a capture to its originating session despite platform privacy limits.** — *Why worth considering:* Instrumenting the two failure modes and evaluating the bet at all depend on attribution that modern ad platforms make harder each year.

**6. Om Coda can distinguish "did not understand" from "understood and did not tap" with a mechanism that does not yet exist.** — *Why worth considering:* The learning design rests on separating these, and unseparated they collapse into one uninformative number.

**7. Om Coda can resist adding persuasion or retargeting to continue-scroll when cost per capture disappoints.** — *Why worth considering:* Organizational discipline is doing load-bearing work here; the doctrine forbids exactly the move a struggling campaign is most tempted to make.

**8. Om Coda can resist offering a sales call when conversion is slow.** — *Why worth considering:* The purity of the growth model depends on the house declining the easiest available fix under pressure.

**9. Om Coda's own campaign methodology exists, is documented, and is genuinely good.** — *Why worth considering:* The prepared workspace *is* the methodology; if the templates are thin, the readiness proof proves the wrong thing.

**10. Om Coda holds real immigration domain expertise, not only software capability.** — *Why worth considering:* Shipping rule packs and immigration-specific sequences is a subject-matter product wearing a software product's clothes.

**11. Om Coda can read public firm facts reliably across a long tail of small-practice websites.** — *Why worth considering:* Forward-deploy specificity depends on it, and small professional sites are frequently thin, script-heavy, or bot-blocked.

**12. Public firm facts are rich enough to make a prepared workspace feel specific rather than generic.** — *Why worth considering:* If the result looks like a template with a logo dropped in, the whole "I prepared this campaign" positioning collapses into a mail-merge.

**13. Om Coda has the standing to stage a workspace under another business's name and branding.** — *Why worth considering:* Using a firm's identity before any agreement exists is a trademark and reputation question, not only a design decision.

**14. A no-login public workspace can be served without leaking or being indexed.** — *Why worth considering:* Unauthenticated URLs holding a named firm's prepared campaign are guessable, shareable and crawlable by default.

**15. Forward-deploy cost stays near zero for the captures that never convert.** — *Why worth considering:* Marginal cost is claimed as a read plus compute, and most captures will be non-converting, so the denominator matters more than the numerator.

**16. Junk, competitor and mischief captures can be filtered before spending compute on them.** — *Why worth considering:* An open one-click capture with no verification invites exactly this, and the model has no gate before the expensive step.

**17. Om Coda can send that first text or email to a captured consultant compliantly.** — *Why worth considering:* Consent for the first agent message is flagged as unresolved, and it is also the single message the entire activation path depends on.

**18. The agent can respond quickly enough, around the clock, to hold an activation conversation.** — *Why worth considering:* An agent walking beside someone implies availability, and a delayed reply is what turns a warm capture cold.

**19. The agent is genuinely automated rather than a human quietly doing the walkthrough.** — *Why worth considering:* If a person does the narration, the model is operator-led growth described as agent-led, and its cost structure is completely different.

**20. Activation can hold many concurrent conversations without a human bottleneck appearing.** — *Why worth considering:* The economics only work at volume, and volume is where a presentation-only agent is most likely to need rescuing.

**21. Om Coda can stand up a real escrow arrangement — vehicle, provider, and legal structure.** — *Why worth considering:* Escrow is the only money door, and holding third-party commercial funds is a regulated activity in its own right.

**22. Those terms are enforceable across the provinces and countries the firms sit in.** — *Why worth considering:* A contingent agreement is only as good as the ability to collect on it when the outcome is met.

**23. Om Coda can measure the outcome that releases escrow to a standard a firm will accept.** — *Why worth considering:* Attribution disputes at release are the predictable failure mode of any outcome-based commercial term.

**24. Om Coda can build database connections across a fragmented practice-software market — or a plain export is genuinely enough.** — *Why worth considering:* This is the difference between one integration and dozens, and the docs deliberately leave the definition open while the activation finish line depends on it.

**25. Om Coda can hold firm database credentials safely and prove it to a professional buyer.** — *Why worth considering:* Storing the authorization is house work, and the security answer arrives before the trust does.

**26. Om Coda can process end-client personal data once authorization lands, under whatever agreement the firm's obligations require.** — *Why worth considering:* Forward-deploy avoids client data, and everything after authorization is entirely made of it.

**27. Om Coda's own brand does not need to be known for the workspace to carry the conversation.** — *Why worth considering:* Cold credibility is substituted with demonstrated preparation, which is a real bet about how professionals evaluate unknown vendors.

**28. Firms will not read Om Coda as a competitor or as an intermediary encroaching on their client relationships.** — *Why worth considering:* A vendor that holds the book, writes the outreach and owns the methodology looks structurally like a channel competitor.

**29. Om Coda can maintain Canadian immigration reference data correctly and continuously as house supply.** — *Why worth considering:* This is an ongoing editorial obligation with a liability tail, funded by nothing in the money model.

**30. Om Coda accepts the liability exposure of shipped rule packs.** — *Why worth considering:* Ownership is flagged as open, but the operating position in the meantime is that the house carries it by default.

**31. Om Coda can support running firms operationally without naming a support function.** — *Why worth considering:* Every seat is accounted for except the one that answers a running client's question at 4pm.

**32. Someone at Om Coda will actually read running-firm friction and turn it into documented gaps.** — *Why worth considering:* The evolution facet is named and empty; without a staffed reader it stays empty by construction.

**33. The Register methodology overhead pays for itself rather than slowing the product below market speed.** — *Why worth considering:* Discipline is treated as a pure good, and its cost is the calendar.

**34. Om Coda can run Tower alongside its other products without Tower needing a dedicated team.** — *Why worth considering:* Several house obligations here — data currency, agent operation, support, escrow admin — are continuous rather than project-shaped.

**35. The assisted door will not quietly become the default because it is easier and converts better.** — *Why worth considering:* Keeping both doors open is honest and also creates a gradient the house will feel every time agent-led growth underperforms.

**36. Om Coda can close the prototype-to-production gap in a relevant timeframe.** — *Why worth considering:* Nothing in acquisition or activation is built, and persistence is session-scoped, so the first real firm arrives at an unbuilt system.

**37. Om Coda can turn away or exit a firm whose outreach is improper.** — *Why worth considering:* Refusing improper outreach is assigned to the consultant, which leaves the house with no stated remedy when the consultant does not.

**38. Om Coda will not be treated as responsible for the immigration advice its rules imply.** — *Why worth considering:* A vendor supplying eligibility determinations to regulated professionals is a plausible target when an applicant is harmed.

**39. Om Coda can hold the prepared workspace warm long enough to re-approach someone who abandoned.** — *Why worth considering:* Abandonment is recorded as a state with an instrumentation transition and no recovery path, and most captures will land there.

**40. Om Coda can experiment on ads without the experiment itself teaching competitors the play.** — *Why worth considering:* Feed ads are public artifacts, and the ad is the most copyable part of the whole approach.

**41. Contingent cost is read by buyers as confidence rather than as a small vendor unable to charge.** — *Why worth considering:* The money posture is meant to remove risk for the buyer; it can equally signal that the product cannot command a price.

**42. One acquired persona in V1 stays one — the house will not need a second Approach mid-experiment.** — *Why worth considering:* If the tapper turns out to be an assistant, an agency, or a partner rather than the authorizing consultant, the single-Approach rule quietly stops holding.
